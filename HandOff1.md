## 1. Project History and the Pivot

### The Original Theory and Assumptions
This project builds on Baldi et al. (2025), *A Theory of Synaptic Neural Balance: From Local to Global Order*. For neural networks with positively homogeneous activation functions ($f(\lambda x) = \lambda f(x)$ for $\lambda > 0$, such as ReLU) and compatible graph topology, multiplying a hidden unit's incoming weights and bias by $\lambda > 0$ while dividing its outgoing weights by $\lambda$ preserves the exact input-output mapping of the network. While balancing alone does not alter immediate training or test accuracy at the moment of transformation, interleaving it with gradient descent acts as a function-preserving optimization intervention that reshapes subsequent gradient updates (including scaling the effective stateless-SGD step size on a weight matrix scaled by $t$ as $\eta_{\text{eff}} = \eta / t^2$). Remark 9.11 of the published paper explicitly notes that dropout and synaptic balancing can be interleaved during training, and Section 12.2 highlights balancing alongside dropout as a general training intervention.

### Completed Preliminary Direction: Two-Hidden-Layer MLP & SNN Proxy Study
Based on Section 12.4 of Baldi et al. (2025), we first ran a 1-seed, 10-epoch diagnostic smoke test (`seed=42`, `v2.2.5`) and then completed a controlled $2 \times 2$ factorial experiment across 5 fresh paired confirmatory seeds (`seeds=43–47`, `v2.2.8`) on a two-hidden-layer fully connected `784-256-128-10` ReLU network trained on MNIST for 60 epochs with stateless SGD (`lr = 0.001`), followed by ANN-to-SNN conversion:
1. **ANN Optimization Interaction (`seeds=43–47`):** Without balancing, dropout reduced 60-epoch ANN test accuracy by `-1.34` percentage points (`89.66%` to `88.32%`). When interleaved with once-per-epoch partial balancing, the dropout penalty shrank to `-0.20` percentage points (`90.85%` to `90.65%`), yielding a positive factorial interaction ($\Delta_{\text{interaction}} = +1.146$ percentage points, 95% CI `[+0.88, +1.41]` pp, unadjusted $p = 0.00023$). Interleaved balancing also accelerated validation learning curves across all checkpoints (for `Group D` vs. `Group B`: `+18.10` pp at Epoch 10 [`72.70%` vs. `54.60%`], `+5.59` pp at Epoch 30 [`87.25%` vs. `81.66%`], and `+2.05` pp at Epoch 60 [`89.85%` vs. `87.80%`]) and converged to a normalized balance deficit of $\approx 10^{-6}$ with all 384 hidden neurons remaining balance-eligible.
2. **SNN Software Operation-Count Proxy Results (`seeds=43–47`):** Under tied layer-wise 99.9th-percentile recalibrated thresholds at $T = 50$ timesteps, the combined dropout + balance condition (`Group D`) achieved a statistically significant factorial interaction in hidden event-count SynOps/sample ($\Delta_{\text{interaction}} = -17,202.26$ hidden SynOps/sample, 95% CI `[-22,980.65, -11,423.87]`, $p = 0.001169$), reducing hidden operations by `-6,856.84` SynOps/sample (`-2.83%`) relative to dropout alone (`Group B`) while improving converted SNN test accuracy by `+2.29` percentage points. *(Note: These are software operation-count proxies, not physical hardware energy measurements in joules.)*

### Near-Term Priority: Standard Convolutional Networks with Shared Weights
Note that Section 10 of Baldi et al. (2025) already evaluated CIFAR-10 using *locally connected* layers (without shared convolutional kernels). We are keeping the SNN conversion work frozen as a completed preliminary phase (until neuromorphic hardware access is available) and prioritizing a distinct ANN optimization question: **How does interleaved synaptic balancing interact with dropout in standard Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) with shared weights, and how does that interaction depend on the balancing granularity and cost domain?**

## 2. Theoretical Challenge: CNN Weight Sharing, Pooling Blocks & Cost Domains

Section 8.3 of the published Baldi et al. (2025) paper discusses why standard convolutional weight sharing complicates individual neuronal balancing: all spatial neurons in an output feature-map channel share the same convolutional kernel parameters. Furthermore, Appendix B proves convergence for generic tied-layer balancing in standard layered feedforward networks; it does not automatically establish convergence for architectures combining shared convolutional kernels, spatial max-pooling, and dropout.

1. **CNN Balancing Modes (Section 8.3 vs. Section 8.2):**
   * **Per-Channel / Filter Balancing (`balance_mode="channel"`, `fc_balance_mode="neuron"` — Primary CNN Method, Section 8.3 First Approach):** Applies one shared scaling factor $\lambda_{\ell,c} > 0$ across all spatial neurons in convolutional channel $c$ (with a distinct factor for each channel) and balances individual neurons in the FC hidden layer (`608` total constraints in our 6-layer CNN). *(Note: The second Section 8.3 CNN approach—computing distinct multipliers per spatial neuron and averaging kernel weights—alters the network's forward mapping and is not used here.)*
   * **Whole-Layer Balancing (`balance_mode="layer"`, `fc_balance_mode="layer"` — Coarser Comparison, Section 8.2 Generic Tied-Layer Balancing):** Applies a single shared scalar $\lambda_\ell > 0$ across the entire hidden layer (`5` layer-transition constraints).
2. **Pooling Commutativity & Coordinated Convolution-Plus-Pooling Block Definition:** While `MaxPool2d` is not invariant to arbitrary per-location scaling ($\max(\lambda_1 a_1, \lambda_2 a_2) \neq \lambda \max(a_1, a_2)$), it is strictly positively homogeneous whenever all spatial positions inside each pooling window share the same positive multiplier $\lambda > 0$. Both `channel` mode and `layer` mode satisfy this condition. When a convolutional layer is immediately followed by spatial pooling (`Conv -> ReLU -> MaxPool2d -> NextLayer`), the convolution and pooling operation are defined as one **coordinated function-preserving block**: its incoming cost sums over the pre-pooling convolutional connections/parameters, and its outgoing cost sums over the post-pooling learned connections into the next parameterized layer.
3. **Parameter-Space $L_2$ vs. Expanded Connection-Graph $L_2$ Cost Domains:**
   * **Unique-Parameter Domain (`cost_domain="parameter"`):** Defines $A^{\text{param}} = \|W^{(\ell)}\|_F^2 + \|b^{(\ell)}\|_2^2$ and $B^{\text{param}} = \|W^{(\ell+1)}\|_F^2$, minimizing the sum of squared unique stored parameters.
   * **Expanded Connection-Graph Domain (`cost_domain="connection_graph"`):** Our operationalization of aggregate connection-level synaptic cost in a weight-shared CNN, weighting each shared kernel tap $(u,v)$ by its exact valid non-padded spatial multiplicity $M_\ell^{\text{in}}(u,v)$ (e.g., for a $3 \times 3$ kernel with `padding=1`, center taps have multiplicity $HW$, edge taps have $H(W-1)$ or $(H-1)W$, and corner taps have $(H-1)(W-1)$, summing to $8,836$ at $32 \times 32$ and $2,116$ at $16 \times 16$) and biases by $HW$. Under Kaiming initialization ($\mathbb{E}[R^{\text{param}}] = 1,236.604$, $\mathbb{E}[R^{\text{graph}}] = 309,919.085$), parameter-domain balancing scales early convolutional layers up and `fc1` down, whereas connection-graph balancing scales early convolutional layers down and `fc1` up. Both domain deficits and per-layer squared costs are logged at every epoch.

## 3. Research Hypotheses

* **H1 (CNN Factorial Interaction):** Interleaved synaptic balancing alters the effect of dropout in standard convolutional networks with shared weights on CIFAR-10, quantified by the primary difference-in-differences estimator $\Delta_{\text{interaction}} = (D - C) - (B - A)$ alongside the simple effect $D - B$ and comparison against $A$ and $C$.
* **H2 (Conditional 1D Validation Dropout Slices):** Interleaved balancing shifts the best validation dropout probability along conditional 1D sweeps over convolutional dropout (holding FC dropout fixed) and FC dropout (holding convolutional dropout fixed).
* **H3 (Generalization Across Complexity):** The interaction between dropout and synaptic balancing persists across more complex image-classification tasks and architectures.

## 4. Staged Execution Plan & Statistical Protocol

1. **Stage 1 (Mathematical & Numerical Verification Gate):** Verify exact logit invariance in `float64` (`< 1e-12`) and `float32` (`< 1e-5`) across all 8 `(cost_domain, balance_mode, fc_balance_mode)` combinations (including active fixed-mask dropout), independent brute-force and `F.unfold` graph-cost verification (`< 1e-9` per layer and full network) with forward-pass output-shape checks, per-transition parameter-vs-graph multiplier divergence, zero-cost unit accounting, and paired augmentation/dropout RNG isolation (`--stage 1`), persisting all results to `stage1_verification_report.json`.
2. **Stage 2 (CIFAR-10 $2 \times 2$ Factorial Experiment):**
   * **Exploratory Smoke (`--mode smoke`, `seed=42`, 5 epochs) & Validation Pilot (`--mode pilot`, `seeds=43–47`, 60 epochs):** Strictly withhold the official 10,000-image CIFAR-10 test set (`test_loader=None`). Use validation curves (`val_accuracy`, `val_loss`) and the unaugmented training-subset evaluation (`train_subset_eval_accuracy`) to verify whether 60 epochs of stateless SGD sufficiently train the CNN and to lock the primary balancing configuration before touching the test set.
   * **Confirmatory Run (`--mode full`, `seeds=48–55`, 60 epochs):** Unlock the official 10,000-image CIFAR-10 test set on 8 disjoint seeds after all architecture and optimizer settings are frozen. *(Statistical caveat: paired 95% confidence intervals and $p$-values across seeds quantify training, initialization, shuffling, and dropout randomness conditional on the fixed CIFAR-10 data split.)*
3. **Stage 3 (Mechanistic Controls & H2 Validation Sweep):** Compare `C_ref` vs. `C_rand` (Dropout Off) and `D_ref` vs. `D_rand` (Dropout On) under matched random function-preserving rescaling, reporting symmetric paired statistics (mean, SD, SE, 95% CI, $t$-statistic, and unadjusted $p$-value) for $\delta_C$, $\delta_D$, and $\delta_{\text{interaction}} = \delta_D - \delta_C$, alongside conditional 1D validation sweeps over dropout rates. Stage 3 strictly withholds the official test set across all modes unless `--stage3-confirmatory-test-eval` is explicitly passed.
4. **Stage 4 (Architectural Scale-Up):** Extend to BatchNorm, residual skip connections, and CIFAR-100 / ImageNet subsets only after Stages 1–3 are established.
